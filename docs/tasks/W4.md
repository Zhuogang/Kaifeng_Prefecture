Do only your section. The lead compared all nine shots. Shots 2 and 7 have new cameras set by the lead; don't move them. Keep budgets and gates passing.

## L: Sky and exposure (biggest global fault)
The zenith is **navy** in nearly every shot (1, 3, 5, 8). In `ref1`, `ref5`, `ref8` it's a pale, clean, lighter blue, grading to warm peach at the horizon. A dark saturated sky makes everything look over-processed, like a polarised tourist photo. Raise sky luminance and reduce its saturation a lot at the zenith in `morning` and `sunset`. Then rebalance exposure so midtones are bright and warm like the refs: shots read underexposed. Check `node tools/colorstats.mjs` against refs 1, 5, 8. Determinism holds.

## A: Paving colour, the real cause of the "sand" (avenue, also `src/materials/paving.js`)
Paving has a **tan albedo under an already warm sun**, so it's warm twice, which is why shots 2, 7 and 8 read as sand. `ref1`, `ref2`, `ref7` show **neutral grey stone and grey brick** warmed only by the light. Make paving albedo close to neutral grey, slightly cool, with low-saturation stone-to-stone variation. Then judge shot 2's new camera: lit lane in the foreground with visible bricks, vermilion fence, canal, trees and the far side. Shot 7's new camera is in the **west** corridor looking south, where the morning sun streams in from the left and blossom shows on the open side, per `ref7`: check it's in the corridor at a sensible height and clear of columns (you may nudge x and z by a few metres only), and add the blossom rows if they're not visible from it.
